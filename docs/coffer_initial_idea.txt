Yes. Coffers has the potential to be much more than an expense tracker. The interesting part is the idea that the app builds a financial memory of the user and then uses that history to help them make better decisions.

I would frame the product around:

Coffers — Know where your money went. Know where it's going.

The core loop becomes:

Money comes in → user plans → user spends → Coffers observes → analyzes → remembers → helps with the next decision.

Let's brainstorm the product before we touch technology.

1. First: What Coffers actually is

I wouldn't position it primarily as a "budgeting app."

I'd make it a personal financial decision system.

Traditional expense tracker:

"You spent K250 on food."

Coffers:

"You've spent K1,420 on food this month. At your current rate, you'll likely spend around K2,050 by month-end. Your food spending is 23% higher than your average over the last three months."

And eventually:

"You received K5,000 today. Based on your previous spending patterns and your current commitments, allocating K1,500 to savings would leave approximately K3,500 available for the month."

That's the direction that makes the application interesting.

18. Coffers should eventually learn patterns

But don't start with AI.

Build the data foundation first.

After enough history, Coffers can identify:

Recurring income

Recurring expenses

Rent usually occurs between the 1st and 3rd.

Spending patterns

You spend significantly more on weekends.

Category trends

Food spending has increased for three consecutive months.

Forgotten expenses

You usually buy electricity around the 15th.

Cash-flow pressure

Your balance tends to fall sharply during the last week of the month.

Financial habits

Most of your discretionary spending occurs within five days after payday.

That last one could be extremely useful.
